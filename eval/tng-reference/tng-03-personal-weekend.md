## Raw Script

So it's Sunday evening, and I just wanted to do a little check-in about the weekend because it was actually really good and I don't want to just rush past it into the week without acknowledging that.

Saturday started a bit slow. Lucas was up way too early, I think it was just after six, and he was already excited about birds. He's been on this bird watching thing ever since we went to Briar Ridge Reserve about three weeks ago, and he got a starter bird book from the library and now he wants to go again. So we talked Isabel into it and we decided to just make a morning of it. Pack some lunch, head over to the reserve, see what we could find.

Isabel made sandwiches and filled up water bottles and we got out of the house by nine which honestly for us with two kids on a Saturday morning is a genuine achievement. Sofia was not particularly enthused. She's at the age where going on a nature walk with her parents is mildly embarrassing even though there's no one around to be embarrassed in front of. But she came along and by the time we actually got into the reserve she was fine. She found a really interesting looking mushroom and spent about twenty minutes photographing it from different angles, which, I'm not going to pretend I understand the aesthetic she was going for, but she was engaged and happy.

Lucas was in his element. He'd brought his library book and he was cross-referencing every bird he saw, which meant we moved very slowly. We maybe walked two kilometers in two and a half hours. But it was genuinely lovely. He identified, or at least plausibly identified, four different species: a great tit, what he was very confident was a nuthatch though I'm less certain, a couple of wood pigeons which everyone knows, and then something he called a redstart which would have been unusual for this area and I think might have been wishful thinking. But I didn't want to tell him that because he was so pleased.

Isabel had made it clear she was the official picnic coordinator and she pulled off an extremely good picnic, which is impressive because I know she was quite busy this week. She had packed not just sandwiches but also those little cheese triangles that Lucas loves, and a flask of proper coffee for us, and chocolate biscuits. We sat in the picnic area near the pond and it was one of those mornings where the light is really nice and everything just feels calm. I noticed I wasn't thinking about work at all which is honestly not always a given for me on a Saturday morning.

The one mild disaster was the sunscreen. She had definitely mentioned to me on Friday evening that we needed to bring sunscreen. I was the one who ended up packing the bag on Saturday morning and I did not put the sunscreen in. So by noon we were all slightly pink and had to head home earlier than we might have otherwise. There were no consequences beyond being a bit pink and Isabel being entirely justified in saying I told you so, which she did, once, and then let it go, which I appreciate.

Sunday was a different kind of good. I went for a run first thing in the morning, which was the first proper run I'd done in about three weeks. I've been saying I would start running again and then not doing it. So I'm glad I actually went. It was about forty-five minutes, not fast, I don't have a pace to report that I'd be proud of. But I felt good afterward. My body remembered how to do it even if it was a bit surprised to be asked. I came back, had breakfast with Isabel while the kids were still asleep, and we had one of those quiet conversations that feel like a luxury compared to the usual weekday rush.

In the evening I called Beverly. We try to speak every couple of weeks because we've both been bad at keeping up with old friends and we made a bit of a pact about it. Beverly is a GP now, she's been doing general practice for four years, and she mentioned that she's been seriously thinking about moving to private practice. She's been having this ongoing internal debate about it. The NHS side of her says you stay where you're needed, but the private practice offer she had come through apparently has some real appeal and she's tired of the system constraints that make it hard to do the actual medical work. I mostly listened. It's not really a question where outside advice is useful, it's more that she needed to talk it through. But I did say, whatever you decide, I think you know what you need. She laughed and said that's very unhelpfully accurate.

I've been thinking after the call about how rare it is to have that kind of friend. The one who you can not see for six weeks and then talk for ninety minutes and it's completely natural. I have good colleagues at Starfleet Analytics, I like the people I work with, but that depth of comfort is different. I should probably call Beverly more often than every couple of weeks. At least one of those calls a month should be longer than the usual thirty minutes.

One small resolution from the weekend: I want to try to protect at least one afternoon a week from my phone. Not turn it off, just put it in a drawer somewhere and do something else. Saturday at the reserve I'd left it in the bag and I genuinely didn't notice its absence for maybe three hours. That felt right. I want more of that.

Good weekend. The kind that doesn't sound impressive but makes the weeks feel sustainable.

## Polished Version

A good weekend, worth taking a moment to record before the week starts.

Saturday we went to Briar Ridge Reserve. Lucas has been in a bird watching phase since we first went three weeks ago and he's been asking to return ever since. We packed a picnic and got out of the house by nine — a genuine Saturday morning achievement with two kids. Sofia came along without much enthusiasm initially but ended up happily photographing a mushroom from many angles near the trail. Lucas moved at about two kilometers per two and a half hours, cross-referencing every bird against his library book. He identified a great tit, what he was confident was a nuthatch, a couple of wood pigeons, and something he called a redstart — possibly wishful thinking, but I didn't say so.

Isabel organized an excellent picnic: sandwiches, Lucas's favourite cheese triangles, a flask of coffee, and chocolate biscuits. We sat by the pond in good morning light, and I noticed I wasn't thinking about work at all. The one mild disaster: I had been explicitly told to pack sunscreen on Friday evening and I forgot. Everyone came home slightly pink, Isabel said I told you so once and then let it go.

Sunday I finally went for the run I'd been promising myself for three weeks. Forty-five minutes, nothing remarkable pace-wise, but I felt good afterward. Breakfast with Isabel while the kids slept was one of those rare quiet moments.

In the evening I called Beverly. She's a GP and has been seriously considering a move to private practice — the ongoing tension between staying where she's needed and the constraints of the current system versus a private offer that has real appeal. I mostly listened. She said my response — "whatever you decide, I think you know what you need" — was unhelpfully accurate, which made her laugh.

After the call I was thinking about how rare it is to have a friendship where six weeks of silence collapses into a ninety-minute conversation that feels completely natural. I want to make at least one of those calls per month longer than thirty minutes.

One small resolution: protect one phone-free afternoon per week. At the reserve on Saturday I'd left the phone in the bag and didn't notice its absence for three hours. That felt right.
