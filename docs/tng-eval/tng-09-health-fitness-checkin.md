## Raw Script

So I want to do a quick health check-in because I had my annual appointment with Beverly last week and I've been processing what came out of it and I think I want to say some of it out loud.

The appointment went well in the sense that nothing alarming was flagged. Beverly did a full panel and the main thing she pointed at was cholesterol, specifically LDL, which is slightly elevated. Not dangerously elevated, not in a we-need-to-have-a-serious-conversation territory, just above the line. She said, and I'm paraphrasing, you're not in trouble but this is the time to change things, before you are. And I appreciated her directness. We've been friends long enough that she doesn't soften things unnecessarily. She gave me the specific numbers, she explained what they mean in practical terms, she said the primary lever is diet and exercise, and if I actually do both for six months and the number doesn't improve then we'd consider medication. But she really doesn't think it'll come to that.

So I came home from that appointment and talked to Isabel about it. Isabel is one of those people who, when faced with a challenge, immediately starts problem-solving. She didn't let me sink into it. She said, let's do it together, and she meant it. We've decided to run three times a week together, at least twice a week as a pair if schedules allow. She's also rethinking some of what we cook, specifically reducing the things that are high in saturated fat. She hasn't made it feel like a diet so much as a recalibration. I appreciate that a lot. It doesn't feel punitive.

The first week of this new routine was reasonable. I went for a run on Tuesday and Thursday and Isabel came both times. The pace was slow. Genuinely slow. I am not a fast runner right now and I have accepted that. I think what I care about is the consistency rather than the performance. I've been here before, I know how this goes. You start slow, you feel ridiculous, and then after six or eight weeks you're not slow anymore and you feel less ridiculous. Tuesday's run was about thirty-five minutes, Thursday's was slightly longer, about forty, because I was feeling better on the second one.

I also did yoga once this week. There's a thirty-minute video I've had saved for probably three months and I finally did it on Wednesday morning. My flexibility is, let me say, not what it once was. The tree pose I was attempting was more of a shrub. But I did the thirty minutes and I felt better afterward so that's going in the good column.

Beverly also mentioned sleep. I've had the Tricorder Health wearable for about eight months and I've been ignoring what it says because I didn't love the numbers. The average it's been recording is around six point two hours. Beverly said she'd like to see that closer to seven and a half. Six point two to seven and a half is an hour and twenty minutes of additional sleep, which sounds modest written down but is quite significant in practice when you have two kids and a commute and a job that involves things that need to get done in the evenings sometimes.

I've identified two behaviors that are directly costing me sleep and that are within my control. The first is that I routinely stay up past eleven watching things I'm only half interested in, just because I don't have the energy to actively decide to stop. The second is that I look at work notifications in the evening, which sometimes creates a low-grade background anxiety that makes it harder to fall asleep even when I go to bed at a reasonable time. For the first one, I've set a soft cutoff on the media system at ten-thirty. I can override it but I have to actively choose to. For the second one, I've turned off work notifications after eight in the evening. These feel like small changes but they add up.

The thing I keep coming back to from the Beverly conversation is the phrase she used: this is the time to change things, before you are in trouble. There's a version of this that I understand logically but haven't really internalized. Like, I know that physical health has a compounding character. If you're in good habits at forty it's much easier to be in good habits at fifty. If you're not, catching up is harder than maintaining. I know this. And I've still been making the choices that don't serve that knowledge. So the cholesterol thing is useful. Not in a crisis way, just in a wake-up call way.

There's also something about the connection between physical health and the quality of my work that I want to name. I've noticed in the past few months, the months that were heavily crunched at work, that my focus is worse by mid-afternoon, my patience in meetings is lower, my creativity is lower. And I've been blaming that on workload. But I think part of it is physical. Not enough sleep, not enough movement, too much coffee as a substitute for both. When I think back to periods where I was running regularly and sleeping reasonably well, those are the same periods where I remember feeling most effective at work. That's not a coincidence.

Goal for the next six months: run three times a week, yoga once or twice a week, sleep average above seven hours, coffee at most two cups before noon. Beverly is going to do a follow-up panel in six months and I'll have numbers to look at. I'm treating this as the same kind of serious that I would treat a project deadline. Which is a slightly strange thing to say about your own body but here we are.

Good check-in. Onwards.

## Polished Version

An annual appointment with Beverly Crusher prompted this health check-in.

The results were broadly good — nothing alarming — but LDL cholesterol came back slightly elevated. Beverly's framing: not in trouble yet, and this is exactly the right time to change things. The recommended interventions are diet and exercise; if six months of genuine effort doesn't move the number, medication would be considered. She doesn't expect it to come to that.

I talked to Isabel afterward and she immediately offered to do it together, which made all the difference. We've started running three times a week (twice together when schedules align) and Isabel is recalibrating the cooking toward less saturated fat. First week: two thirty-five to forty-minute runs, one yoga session (flexibility is currently closer to a shrub than a tree). Starting slow, which is fine — consistency matters more than pace.

Sleep is the other flag. The Tricorder Health wearable has been recording an average of 6.2 hours; Beverly wants 7.5. Two identified behaviors are costing the sleep: staying up past eleven watching things I'm only half-interested in, and checking work notifications in the evening (which creates low-grade background anxiety). Changes: a soft ten-thirty media cutoff that requires active override, and work notifications off after eight.

The bigger realization from the appointment is intellectual knowledge versus internalized behavior. I've known for years that physical health compounds — good habits at forty make fifty much easier. I've still been making the bad choices. The elevated LDL is a useful, non-crisis wake-up call.

There's also a direct connection to work quality: the months with the heaviest crunch and least exercise are the same months where mid-afternoon focus, meeting patience, and creativity were worst. That's not purely workload — it's physical.

Six-month goal: run three times per week, yoga once or twice weekly, sleep average above seven hours, coffee capped at two cups before noon. Beverly will run a follow-up panel in six months.
